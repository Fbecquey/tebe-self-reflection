The Business Mirror

An honest look at what you already know but haven't faced yet.

There are things in every business that the owner knows. Not suspects. Knows. And hasn't looked at directly.

Not because they're unintelligent or avoidant by nature. But because looking costs something. It means deciding. It means admitting that something isn't working, that a direction was wrong, that a pattern has been running longer than it should have.

This is not a questionnaire. There are no right answers here, no framework to complete, no score at the end. This page is simply a place to write, without the audience of a spreadsheet, a coach, or a plan.

What you write belongs to you. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Start anywhere. The thing you keep not scheduling. The number you haven't looked at in three months. The offer you know isn't working. The conversation you've been meaning to have. The part of the business you're quietly building around instead of through.

Write until you find the sentence you didn't plan to write. That's usually the one that matters.

If you'd rather do this by hand, print this page.

There's a reason handwriting surfaces things that typing doesn't. When you write by hand, your brain processes at a slower speed, which means your thoughts have to form more deliberately before they reach the page. The internal editor — the one that tidies your thinking before it becomes visible — has less time to intervene.

Studies on expressive writing consistently show that handwriting produces more emotionally honest and specific output than typing. The friction is the point. It slows you down enough to catch what you were about to skip over.
